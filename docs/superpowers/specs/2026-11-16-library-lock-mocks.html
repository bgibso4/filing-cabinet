Library Lock

One Face ID opens the whole library. Scanning from Control Center, the Action button and Siri never waits for it. Off unless you turn it on.

What's decided

  • Setting“Lock with Face ID” in Settings, off by default.Ben
  • UnlockingFace ID, falling back to the phone's passcode when Face ID isn't working.Ben
  • Locks againAt every launch, and after 5 minutes away from the app. A quick trip to Messages doesn't ask again.Ben
  • Scanning while lockedThe Scan control, the Action button and “Scan a document” open the camera straight away. Save or Discard returns to the cover. A scan never unlocks the library.Ben
  • SpotlightNo scans in Spotlight while the lock is on; they come back when it's turned off.Ben
  • “Find a scan”, Spotlight resultsWait for Face ID, then open. Dropped if Face ID is cancelled.my call
  • NotificationsWhile locked on, cleanup notes give a count, not scan titles: they show on the Lock Screen.my call
  • SheetsWhen the library locks, Share, Mail and Details close first; the app switcher always shows the cover.my call
  • Turning it offAsks for Face ID, so someone holding an open library can't switch it off.my call
  • No passcodeThe switch is disabled. If the passcode is removed later, the lock pauses; nothing is lost.Ben
  • Storage (#93)File protection stays the iOS default. Scans stay in iCloud Backup, the only way back after a lost phone until sync. The privacy policy says so and suggests Advanced Data Protection.Ben
  • DroppedA per-scan lock, number stripping, iOS's own app lock (it removes the Control Center Scan control, as Ben saw on his phone).

Mock 1 · The lock cover

Same ground as Home, the app's icon and name. Face ID starts by itself the moment the app opens: no tap, and the cover lifts as soon as your face is read. The Unlock button only appears if Face ID was cancelled or failed. Nothing of the library shows, including in the app switcher.

Its own group, after Apple Intelligence and Notifications and before Send feedback. The footnote says what still works and points to Apple's two protections.
