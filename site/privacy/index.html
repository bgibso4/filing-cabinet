Privacy

Filing Cabinet · Last updated November 20, 2026

Filing Cabinet keeps your documents on your iPhone. It does not collect anything about you or your scans. It sends only anonymous counts of how the app is used, never your scans or anything in them. A scan leaves the app only when you share it yourself.

Anonymous usage counts

There is no sign-up and no account, no tracking and no advertising.

The app sends anonymous counts of what happens in it (for example, that a scan was saved, or that something failed) to TelemetryDeck, an analytics service. Each count comes with basic details of the device: the iPhone model, the iOS and app versions, the language and region, and settings such as text size. It also comes with a scrambled identifier for this installation of the app, so that counts from one iPhone can be told apart from another's. The identifier is scrambled on your iPhone before it is sent and again by TelemetryDeck, and it isn't connected to your name, your Apple Account or anything else about you. The counts never include your scans, their text or titles, and they aren't used to track you.

Your scans

Scans, and the text, titles and search words the app works out from them, are stored on your iPhone in the app's own storage. Your recent searches and recently viewed scans are kept there too. If your iPhone has a passcode, iOS encrypts this storage with a key tied to it.

All of this is included in your iCloud Backup if you use it. Apple can read an iCloud Backup unless you turn on Advanced Data Protection, which makes it end-to-end encrypted. We suggest turning it on.

Face ID lock

Settings has a "Lock with Face ID" switch. It is off by default. When it is on, the library is covered until Face ID unlocks it, or your iPhone's passcode when Face ID isn't working. It locks each time the app starts, and again after the app has been away for 5 minutes. The lock needs a passcode on your iPhone; without one it is paused.

The lock hides the library in the app. It does not add encryption of its own, and does not protect a backup.

While the lock is on, your scans are removed from iPhone search, and notes about cleaned-up scans stop naming them. Scanning from Control Center, the Action button or Siri still works without unlocking.

Face ID is handled by iOS. The app only learns whether it worked, yes or no. It never sees your face data.

Deleting

A scan you delete, or one that reaches the end of its time in the library, goes to Trash and is permanently deleted 30 days later. You can delete it sooner from Trash. Deleting the app removes everything it stored on your iPhone.

Sharing

When you share a scan, copy its text or save it to Photos, it goes where you send it. What happens to it there is up to that app or person.

Camera

The camera is used only to scan documents.

Photos

The app asks for add-only access to Photos, and only when you choose to save a scan there. It cannot see your photo library.

Notifications

Notes about scans that have been cleaned up are scheduled on your iPhone. They do not come from a server.

Reading and suggestions

Reading the text on a scan, and suggesting a title and collection with Apple Intelligence, happen on your iPhone. Your scans are not sent anywhere to do this. If Apple Intelligence is turned off in iPhone Settings, the app makes no suggestions.

iPhone search

So you can find a scan from iPhone search, the app adds each scan's title, its collection and page count, and a small preview image to your iPhone's search index. The index is stored on your iPhone and can't be read while the phone is locked. The text read from your scans is not added, and scans in Trash are removed.

Feedback

Send feedback opens a draft in your mail app. It contains what you write plus the app version and build, the iOS version and your iPhone model. The report from the "library couldn't open" screen also adds the error's details: its domain, code and description. You can read and change it before you send it, and nothing is sent unless you send it. If you do, your email is used only to reply.

Crash reports from Apple

If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in iPhone Settings, Apple may give us crash and usage reports that do not identify you. They do not contain your scans.
